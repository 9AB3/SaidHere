Privacy

Last updated 22 October 2026

The Wall is designed around anonymous, place-based conversations. You do not create a public account, username, profile, follower list or social graph.

Information The Wall uses

When you use the app, The Wall creates an anonymous session identifier so the service can remember which Walls you have discovered, enforce write access, apply abuse controls and prevent duplicate reports. The service also stores the Walls you visit, messages and replies you submit, reports you make, and technical request information ordinarily required to operate and secure an internet service.

Physical Wall discovery

A Wall is discovered by opening its NFC link. Tapping a valid Wall temporarily unlocks writing for that Wall. The Wall does not require a public profile or precise GPS location to identify the Wall; the physical Wall itself is the discovery mechanism.

Messages and moderation

Messages are intentionally public to people who have discovered that Wall. Posts are stored with an internal anonymous session identifier for abuse prevention and moderation. Users can report inappropriate content, and reported content may be hidden automatically or removed by an administrator.

Local device data

The app may keep a small local cache of Walls you have previously discovered so your history remains useful during temporary connection problems. This data stays on your device unless it is also represented in the service as part of normal Wall discovery history.

What The Wall does not provide

The Wall does not require public names, profile photos, followers, likes or direct messages. The current product is text-only.

Retention and deletion

Wall content is intended to preserve the history of a physical place and may remain available until it is moderated, cleared by an administrator or the Wall is deleted. Anonymous session and visit information may be retained as needed to operate the service, preserve discovered-Wall history and prevent abuse.

Changes

This page will be updated if The Wall materially changes the information it collects or how the service works.
